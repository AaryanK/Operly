# Operly Privacy Policy

**Effective date: August 24, 2026**  
**Last updated: August 24, 2026**

Operly ("Operly," "we," "us," or "our") is an AI-powered operating platform developed and maintained by Dragonzpyder Industries. This Privacy Policy explains how Operly collects, uses, stores, discloses, and protects information when you use Operly websites, accounts, Personal AI, workspaces, Studio, applications, plugins, connectors, APIs, MCP access, agents, automations, and related services (collectively, the "Services").

This policy is intended to describe the Services as they operate today. Individual connectors and enterprise features may present additional notices where a more specific disclosure is appropriate.

## 1. Information We Collect

### 1.1 Account and profile information
We may collect information you provide when creating or managing an account, including your name, email address, authentication information, profile information, workspace memberships, roles, and account preferences.

Passwords are processed as authentication credentials and are stored using one-way password hashing rather than in plaintext.

### 1.2 Workspace and business information
When you create or use an Operly workspace, we may process workspace names, membership and role information, business records, CRM information, operations data, tasks, approvals, configuration, generated applications, Studio projects, files, and other information that you or authorized workspace members provide.

Personal account context and workspace context are separate authorization scopes. A workspace does not automatically receive access to a user's private Personal AI conversation or personal connector data merely because that user belongs to the workspace.

### 1.3 Prompts, conversations, files, and generated content
We may process prompts, messages, instructions, uploaded files, generated files, model responses, workflow state, agent plans, tool results, application source code, and other content needed to provide AI and automation features.

### 1.4 Connector and integration data
If you connect a third-party service, Operly may receive and process information authorized by the permissions you grant. Depending on the connector, this can include account identifiers, profile information, email, calendar events, messages, files, channels, servers, contacts, business profile information, metadata, or other data available through the third-party API.

Operly requests and uses connector permissions to provide user-requested features, agent capabilities, automation, search, retrieval, and authorized actions. The data available to a Personal AI connector and the data available to a workspace connector may be subject to different authorization boundaries.

You can revoke a third-party connection through Operly where supported and may also revoke access through the third-party provider.

### 1.5 Google user data
If you connect a Google account, Operly may access Google user data only within the OAuth scopes you authorize, such as identity information, Gmail data, Google Calendar data, or other Google services that Operly supports.

Operly uses Google user data to provide the features you request, such as searching or summarizing authorized information, creating user-requested context for an AI interaction, or carrying out authorized actions through a Google connector.

Operly does not sell Google user data. Operly does not use Google Workspace API data for advertising. Operly does not permit humans to read Google user data except when necessary for security, abuse investigation, support requested by the user, legal compliance, or where the user has expressly authorized the access.

Operly's use and transfer to any other application of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the **Google API Services User Data Policy**, including the **Limited Use requirements**.

### 1.6 Technical, security, and usage information
We may collect IP address, browser and device information, request metadata, timestamps, session information, security events, API activity, error logs, performance data, model/provider identifiers, token usage, tool invocation metadata, approval events, and audit information. We use this information to operate, secure, debug, improve, and understand the Services.

### 1.7 Cookies and local storage
Operly may use cookies, browser storage, and similar technologies for authentication, session continuity, security, user preferences, and necessary product functionality. If we add non-essential advertising or analytics technologies, we will provide additional notice or controls where required by law.

## 2. How We Use Information

We may use information to:

- provide, maintain, and improve the Services;
- authenticate users and maintain sessions;
- provide Personal AI and workspace functionality;
- run AI models and agent workflows;
- execute plugins, connectors, tools, automations, Studio projects, and user-authorized actions;
- enforce workspace roles, permissions, approvals, and security boundaries;
- remember context or preferences where a memory feature is enabled;
- create, edit, host, or operate user-requested solutions;
- prevent fraud, abuse, unauthorized access, and security incidents;
- troubleshoot errors and provide support;
- measure reliability, performance, and usage;
- comply with law and enforce our agreements; and
- communicate important service, security, or account notices.

## 3. AI Models and Service Providers

Operly may route user content to AI model providers and other infrastructure or service providers when required to perform a requested feature. Depending on the user's configuration, these providers may include models or services operated by third parties or models accessed using user-supplied credentials.

We seek to send only the information reasonably necessary for the requested operation and to respect the active account, workspace, permission, and connector scope. The privacy and data-processing terms of a third-party provider may also apply when you intentionally connect or configure that provider.

Operly does not claim ownership of your prompts, workspace data, files, or other content merely because you use the Services. We do not use private customer content to train a generally available Operly foundation model unless we provide a separate disclosure and obtain any consent required by law or contract.

## 4. Agent Actions, Approvals, and Tool Use

Operly can allow AI agents to read authorized information and, when permitted, take actions through plugins and connectors. Actions may include creating or editing records, sending communications, modifying files, scheduling events, publishing content, or operating user-created solutions.

Operly uses authorization, policy, and approval controls to limit actions according to the active user, workspace, connector, and configured permissions. Certain sensitive or consequential write actions may require explicit approval before execution. No technical control can eliminate every risk, so you should review connector permissions and automation settings appropriate to your use case.

## 5. Personal and Workspace Boundaries

Operly is designed around separate personal and workspace scopes.

- **Personal scope:** belongs to the individual account and may include private conversations, personal preferences, and personal connectors.
- **Workspace scope:** belongs to the relevant workspace and is available according to workspace roles and permissions.

A user's membership in a workspace does not by itself authorize that workspace to read the user's private Personal AI conversation or personal connector content. Conversely, a Personal AI may be allowed to reason across workspaces the user is authorized to access when the user requests or configures that behavior.

## 6. How We Share Information

We may disclose information:

- to service providers that process information for us, such as hosting, database, email-delivery, observability, security, AI model, or infrastructure providers;
- to third-party services you deliberately connect or instruct Operly to use;
- to workspace members according to workspace permissions and the actions you take;
- in connection with a merger, financing, acquisition, reorganization, bankruptcy, or sale of assets, subject to appropriate protections;
- when required by law, legal process, or valid governmental request; or
- when reasonably necessary to protect users, Operly, third parties, or the public from fraud, abuse, security threats, or unlawful activity.

We do not sell personal information for money. If our practices change in a manner that constitutes a "sale," "sharing," or targeted advertising under applicable privacy law, we will provide required notices and choices.

## 7. Data Retention

We retain information for as long as reasonably necessary to provide the Services, maintain account and workspace continuity, satisfy security and audit needs, resolve disputes, enforce agreements, and comply with law.

Retention periods vary by data type and feature. For example, security logs may have a different retention period from workspace content, generated applications, connector tokens, or deleted account records. We may retain limited backup, fraud-prevention, audit, or legal-compliance records after deletion where necessary and permitted by law.

As Operly's connector and enterprise architecture matures, we may publish more specific retention schedules for particular data classes.

## 8. Security

We use administrative, technical, and organizational safeguards designed to protect information. These include access controls, authorization boundaries, secure session controls, credential protection, auditability, and security monitoring appropriate to the Services.

No service can guarantee absolute security. You are responsible for protecting your account credentials, reviewing workspace membership, limiting connector scopes to what you need, and promptly reporting suspected unauthorized access.

Depending on your location and applicable law, you may have rights to request access to, correction of, deletion of, or a copy of personal information, or to object to or restrict certain processing.

You may also be able to:

- disconnect third-party connectors;
- revoke third-party OAuth access directly with the provider;
